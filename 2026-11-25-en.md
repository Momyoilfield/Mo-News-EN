# Mo Daily News · Wednesday, November 25, 2026

## Daily Oil Overview

Oil staged a V-shaped rebound on Tuesday: Brent for January settled at $91.40 a barrel, up 2.3%; WTI for January settled at $82.05, up 2.35%. Both benchmarks fell to three-month lows during the session (Brent $88.10, WTI $79.07) before recovering quickly on fresh reports of attacks on tankers in the Strait of Hormuz. President Trump said he was willing to give US-Iran diplomacy more time, which eased the early slide, but the situation in the strait keeps escalating: Iran says more tankers have hit mines and caught fire, a maritime security body has kept the threat rating at "severe" and warned of further attacks on the Omani side within a day or two. Supply remains ample: Deutsche Bank estimates global inventories rose by 52 million barrels in October, and US commercial crude stocks rose for a second straight week. On OPEC+, Iraq is pressing for a higher assessment of its production capacity, and the full group meets on Sunday, November 29, to decide the 2027 production framework.

## News in Brief

**1. Oil rebounds in a V-shape on Tuesday: Brent settles at $91.40, up 2.3%; WTI at $82.05, up 2.35%, after both hit three-month lows during the session.** Brent for January settled on Tuesday at $91.40 a barrel, up $2.03 (2.3%); WTI for January settled at $82.05, up $1.88 (2.35%). Early in the session both benchmarks fell to three-month lows — Brent $88.10, its lowest since August 27, and WTI $79.07, its lowest since August 10 — before rebounding almost $3 from the lows on the Hormuz tanker reports. Despite the rebound, Brent is still about 1.3% below Friday's close of $92.57, and worries about a supply surplus have not gone away.

**2. Hormuz: Iran says more tankers hit mines and caught fire; maritime body keeps the threat "severe" and shipping through the strait is 65% below pre-war levels.** Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps navy said a tanker exploded and caught fire on Tuesday after striking a mine in restricted waters east of the Strait of Hormuz; Iran's navy separately said two more tankers caught fire after entering a restricted area with their identification systems switched off. None of these claims has been independently verified, and no vessel names, crew details or damage assessments have been released. The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO) confirmed that three vessels were struck by unknown projectiles while transiting eastbound on Sunday, with all crews safe. The Joint Maritime Information Center (JMIC) kept the threat level for the strait at "severe" and said further attacks on tankers using the Omani side were "highly likely" within the next 24 to 48 hours; it recorded seven tanker attacks in the strait between November 16 and 21. Shipping through the strait remains about 65% below pre-war levels, with around 227 vessels waiting at anchorages east of the strait. Iranian Army Commander-in-Chief Amir Hatami said on Tuesday that the strait "will be more closed day by day."

**3. OPEC+: Iraq seeks a higher capacity estimate as Sunday's meeting sets the 2027 framework; the seven core members pumped 2.83 million bpd below quota in October.** Reuters reports that Iraq is pressing OPEC+ to raise its estimate of the country's crude production capacity. OPEC's first-ever independent capacity review assessed Iraq at 4.9 million barrels a day, which Baghdad considers too low; the review will set the baselines for each member's 2027 production targets. All 21 OPEC+ countries meet on Sunday, November 29, to decide the 2027 production framework, and production cuts of about 2 million barrels a day expire at the end of the year. The seven core members produced a combined 28.18 million barrels a day in October, 2.83 million below their quotas, with the shortfall concentrated in Saudi Arabia, Russia and Kuwait. The US Energy Information Administration (EIA) expects a global surplus of 4.05 million barrels a day in 2027.

**4. US inventories rise for a second straight week: commercial crude up 3.4 million barrels to 417.8 million, gasoline up 5.2 million.** EIA data show US commercial crude inventories rose by 3.4 million barrels to 417.8 million in the week ending November 13, a second consecutive weekly build (after a 4.7 million barrel rise the week before), still about 3% below the five-year average; gasoline stocks rose 5.2 million barrels to 215.9 million, about 3% above the five-year average. Deutsche Bank estimates global crude and product inventories rose by 52 million barrels in October, equivalent to a supply surplus of about 1.7 million barrels a day. The American Petroleum Institute's latest weekly report was released on Tuesday evening, and the official EIA report is brought forward to Wednesday morning because of the Thanksgiving holiday.

**5. Regional supply risks spread: Iraq moves to take over Kurdish oilfields, Houthis claim a strike on a Saudi refinery, and the EU plans a 22nd Russia sanctions package.** Iraqi Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi ordered the federal oil ministry to take over oilfields in four provinces under Kurdish security control and oversee the region's oil exports; Kurdish leaders rejected the move, risking a reopening of the decade-long dispute over oil revenues. Yemen's Houthi militias said they had hit Saudi Aramco's 400,000 bpd Rabigh refinery, and Aramco has not loaded any products from the port of Rabigh since Friday. The European Union proposed a 22nd sanctions package against Russia, including a ban on tankers insured in Russia, barring EU firms from servicing Russian energy infrastructure including refineries, and blacklisting more than 70 tankers. US Central Command halted a Pakistan-bound tanker in the Gulf of Oman after a military helicopter fired on it, suggesting vessels carrying Iranian oil and products could increasingly test the US maritime blockade.

**6. US probes cyberattacks on as many as 50 tankers; Senate demands Vitol Venezuela-deal documents from Vance's office.** The Wall Street Journal reports the United States is investigating whether Iran was involved in cyberattacks on as many as 50 energy tankers, some of which also received bomb threats; Iran is the primary suspect and Russia is also seen as a possible culprit, though a Trump administration official said the US has found no evidence of Iranian involvement. Separately, Reuters reports that the US Senate Homeland Security Committee has asked Vice President JD Vance's office to hand over documents on communications with Vitol, the commodities trader, over a Venezuelan oil deal, with a deadline of December 14. The request follows a report that Vance met Vitol trader John Addison before the US operation in Venezuela in January; Vance's office says the meeting never happened. Vitol and Trafigura received special licenses to sell Venezuelan crude after Nicolas Maduro was removed, and those exports have helped ease supply tightness during the Iran war.
