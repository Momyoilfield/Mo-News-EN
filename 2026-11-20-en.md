# Mo Daily News · Friday, November 20, 2026

## Daily Oil Overview

Oil prices fell further on Thursday: expectations of a supply surplus and of higher US interest rates continued to outweigh the war premium from the Strait of Hormuz. Brent for January settled at $90.04 a barrel, down $1.91 (2.1%), after dipping intraday to $88.51; WTI for December settled at $82.87, down $0.39 (0.5%), after an intraday low of $81.66. Brent is still down more than 20% this month, and the Brent-WTI spread narrowed to $7.17 a barrel. In Shenzhen, Trump, Xi and Putin held their first trilateral meeting and issued a four-point "peace declaration" pledging to increase refined fuel production and supply, but it made no mention of the wars in Ukraine or Iran and set out no timetable or figures. The Strait of Hormuz remains effectively closed, with insurance for a single transit now at $64 million. Refining is under strain: global runs in October fell to their lowest seasonal level in recent years, and tanker freight rates in Asia have plunged.

**2. Trump, Xi and Putin hold first trilateral meeting in Shenzhen and issue a four-point "peace declaration" pledging more refined fuel supply, but with no mention of Ukraine or the Iran war.** The three leaders met for about 90 minutes at Kylin Villa in Shenzhen, after Trump held separate bilateral meetings with Putin and Xi. The four-point "Declaration Towards Peace" reaffirmed that "a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought" and promised dialogue on reducing nuclear risks and preventing proliferation; pledged to increase the production and supply of refined petroleum products and keep energy flows stable; promised to advance reform of the United Nations; and sought to limit the jurisdiction of the International Criminal Court and similar bodies over state officials. The declaration made no mention of the war in Ukraine or the war in Iran, and set no timetable, numerical targets or specific volume for the extra fuel output. Putin acknowledged the declaration "did not set ambitious goals"; Trump called the meeting "great" and invited Putin to the G20 summit in Miami in December; China's Foreign Minister Wang Yi said the three sides would keep coordinating at working level. Trump will hold a news conference in Washington at noon on Friday.

**3. Strait of Hormuz still effectively closed: only 6 transits in the November 18 window, and insurance for a single passage now $64 million.** Ship tracker Windward recorded just 6 vessels crossing the Strait of Hormuz in the 24-hour window of November 18 (2 inbound, 4 outbound), about 8% of the pre-crisis level of roughly 73 a day; IMF PortWatch satellite tracking put the average at only 0.4 tankers a day in the week to November 15, 99% below the pre-crisis average. Kpler data put crude and product flows through the strait at about 8.3 million barrels a day in the week to November 11, about 48% of the pre-war level of 17.1 million bpd. A senior US Navy official said on Wednesday that insurance for a single Hormuz transit has soared to $64 million. Iran's navy said three tankers east of the strait were disabled by mines (unverified); Iran says the US has not yet met its conditions for reopening the strait. Trump told Fox News the US needs no help from China or Russia on Iran and claimed the US has complete control of the strait.

**4. Refining under strain: global runs fell to 81.3 million bpd in October, their lowest seasonal level in recent years; Wood Mackenzie cuts Q4 runs forecast by 1.4 million bpd.** International Energy Agency data put global refinery throughput at 81.3 million barrels a day in October, down 1.3 million bpd from the previous month and the lowest seasonal level in recent years: crude is not in short supply — the bottleneck is the capacity to turn it into fuel, hit by attacks on Russian refineries and disrupted crude flows from the Gulf. Wood Mackenzie has cut its forecast for fourth-quarter global crude runs by 1.4 million bpd. Refineries in the Atlantic Basin, including the United States, are running at maximum capacity, but their equipment limits prevent them from offsetting the lost output elsewhere.

**5. Asia tanker freight rates plunge: MR rates on a benchmark Middle East route fall 125 Worldscale points in a day; fewer than six product tankers a day passing Hormuz.** S&P Global reported on November 18 that Medium Range (MR) tanker freight rates dropped by 125 Worldscale points on a benchmark Middle East route, as attacks on commercial vessels near the Strait of Hormuz disrupt refined fuel shipments, leaving more tankers competing for fewer cargoes. S&P Global Commodities at Sea data put daily vessel transits through Hormuz at just 14 on November 9, compared with 32 at the end of October, and brokers estimated fewer than six refined-product tankers were passing through the strait on an average day. The weakness has spread to North Asia, where refined-product exports from China have fallen short of expectations despite the government announcing about 5.65 million metric tons of additional export quotas in late October.

**6. Reuters: Ukrainian drone attacks have cut Russian refineries' combined crude processing by 45% since late October.** Reuters reported on Thursday, citing industry sources, that Ukrainian drone attacks have reduced Russian refineries' combined crude processing volumes by 45% since late October. Ukraine has attacked 13 Russian refineries since the start of November; Rystad Energy data put Russian refinery runs at 4.2 million barrels a day in the first ten days of November, down from 5 million bpd in late October. Russia has urged oil companies to produce lower-grade gasoline, a sign the attacks are affecting refined-product supply.

**7. Outlooks diverge: IEA sees a Q4 surplus of about 1.7 million bpd; Goldman sees Brent averaging about $88 in 2027.** The IEA's November report expects global oil supply to average about 105 million barrels a day in the fourth quarter, exceeding demand by about 1.7 million bpd — the first surplus since the Iran conflict began; global supply rose by 2.3 million bpd in October to 105.4 million bpd, mainly on recovering Gulf exports. Goldman Sachs expects a substantial oversupply in 2027, with Brent averaging roughly $88 for the year and falling to about $78 by year-end if geopolitical risk premia normalise. By contrast, the EIA's Short-Term Energy Outlook of November 10 expects Brent spot prices at around $100 a barrel in November and December.
