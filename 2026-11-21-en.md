# Mo Daily News · Saturday, November 21, 2026

## Daily Oil Overview

Oil prices fell first and then recovered on Friday to settle higher, but still ended the week with a third straight weekly loss: Brent for January settled at $92.57 a barrel, up $0.59 (0.6%); WTI for December settled at $83.98, up $0.77 (0.9%), on its expiry day; the more active January WTI settled at $83.41, up 1.2%. For the week, Brent fell 3.4% and WTI fell 5.2%. Prices dropped sharply intraday, with Brent falling to around $90.33, before recovering in the afternoon on short-covering ahead of the Thanksgiving holiday and worries about the war with Iran. Strong crude exports from the Middle East, with flows through the Strait of Hormuz close to pre-war levels, were the main drag on prices; at the same time, another tanker was attacked and set on fire in the strait on Friday, the Houthis kept striking Riyadh, and President Trump said at a White House news conference that future oil prices depend on "how we settle up with" Iran.

**2. Another tanker attacked and set on fire in the Strait of Hormuz on Friday: an unknown projectile hit an outbound tanker on the port bow; Iran's Revolutionary Guards say the tanker hit a mine.** The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO) agency said on Friday that a tanker sailing outbound through the Strait of Hormuz was struck on its port bow by an unknown projectile about 8 nautical miles north of Khasab, Oman, and caught fire; initial reports said there were no casualties and no environmental impact, the vessel was not identified and the extent of damage was unconfirmed, and the incident occurred at about 15:00 UTC. Iran's Revolutionary Guards said shortly afterwards that a tanker had caught fire after hitting a mine near Khasab — a claim that has not been independently verified. Data compiled by Bloomberg show October was the worst month for tanker attacks since the war began at the end of February, with incidents occurring nearly daily in November; remote-sensing data from Windward show five tankers were struck near Hormuz in the week of November 11–18; ADNOC confirmed one of its vessels was attacked while transiting the strait on the evening of November 17. Vessel transits through the strait fell about 14.6% this week.

**3. Trump holds White House news conference Friday: falling oil prices are tied to Iran, and future prices depend on "how we settle up with" Iran; Iran wants a "fake deal."** President Trump held a news conference at the White House at noon on Friday, flanked by Vice President Vance and Secretary of State Marco Rubio. Trump said oil prices have been dropping and noted that prices are closely tied to how things are going in Iran: "They want to make a deal, but they don't want to make a deal that's acceptable to me. We want to make a real deal, not a fake deal that they've been doing for 51 years." He added that future oil costs "also depend on how we settle up with Iran." Trump acknowledged reports that Iran may be considering attacking the US capital with a so-called "dirty bomb." Vance said at the news conference that the decision on renewed military action rests with the president, and that his job is to support the president. Trump also touted the Shenzhen trilateral summit, while acknowledging it yielded few tangible results.

**4. US media: Trump weighs a new round of extensive strikes on Iran as Israel presses for a four- to seven-day air campaign; deep divisions in the administration, no decision yet.** The New York Times reported on November 19, citing US and Israeli officials briefed on the discussions, that President Trump is weighing plans for another round of extensive military strikes on Iran, taking into account new intelligence about Iran rebuilding its missile and drone arsenals and advancing its chemical and biological weapons programmes; Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu is pressing the United States to join a four- to seven-day campaign of airstrikes. Roman Gofman, the director of Mossad, held meetings in Washington last week, including with CIA Director John Ratcliffe. Military officials said the US has built up a vast arsenal in the region — naval ships, fighter planes and bombers — ready to launch a relatively short campaign of intense strikes against Iran's missile and drone arsenal, energy facilities and Revolutionary Guard command posts, should Trump authorise action. But there are deep differences of opinion within the administration about the strength of the intelligence and the wisdom of assaulting Iran again, and Trump has yet to make a decision. Separately, Reuters reported earlier that Trump told senior aides he may hold off on renewed strikes as long as Iran or its Houthi proxies in Yemen do not provoke the US; Secretary of State Marco Rubio said last week that the war was "militarily effectively over." Qatar is leading mediation efforts, seeking to trade a lifting of the naval blockade and the release of some frozen Iranian funds for Iran accepting IAEA inspectors and reopening the Strait of Hormuz.

**5. Houthi missiles strike Riyadh again: a missile threat on Thursday left debris and material damage; the East-West pipeline was attacked again on Monday.** The Saudi-led coalition said on Thursday it had dealt with a missile threat launched by Yemen's Houthis towards Riyadh, after a loud boom was heard in the capital; an initial assessment showed debris had fallen and material damage had been reported, with the extent still being determined. The coalition also said it had intercepted and destroyed a cruise missile launched by the Houthis towards Saudi Arabia's southern region, and Saudi aircraft carried out airstrikes on the Houthi-controlled Yemeni capital Sanaa early on Thursday. It was the second attack on Riyadh this week: on Tuesday, Saudi air defences intercepted a ballistic missile and a cruise missile over the capital, and a second ballistic missile over the southern region. On Monday, the East-West oil pipeline (Petroline) was attacked again in the Medina region, causing a limited fire that was extinguished, with no injuries reported and pipeline operations unaffected; the Houthis said they had sent five drones against pumping stations on the pipeline. The pipeline runs about 1,200 kilometres from Abqaiq to the Red Sea port of Yanbu and now carries about four million barrels of crude a day, around four per cent of global supply.

**6. Tanker rates bounce: Baltic Dirty Tanker Index at 4,812, up 5% in a day and more than 7% on the week; IEA chief says Hormuz is unlikely ever to return to its pre-war state.** A sharp rise in attacks on ships in the Strait of Hormuz this week has reversed the recent decline in tanker rates: the Baltic Dirty Tanker Index stood at 4,812, up by more than 5% on November 19 and by well over 7% since the start of the week. The uptick is notable but modest against the backdrop of prevailing VLCC rates. In a guest essay for The Economist's "The World Ahead 2027," IEA Executive Director Fatih Birol wrote: "I don't see any credible pathway back to the situation that existed in February 2026. Shipping flows through the Strait of Hormuz have been blocked once, with a hugely disruptive effect on oil and gas markets."
