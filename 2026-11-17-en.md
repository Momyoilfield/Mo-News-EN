# Mo Daily News · Tuesday, November 17, 2026

## Daily Oil Overview

Oil prices tumbled on Monday: Brent crude settled at $92.44 a barrel, down $3.40 (3.6%), and US WTI settled at $85.72, down $2.84 (3.2%), extending the slide of the past three weeks. The International Energy Agency said on Friday that the world oil market will return to a surplus in the fourth quarter, with supply averaging 105 million barrels a day, 1.7 million bpd above demand; Brent has fallen about 30% from its early-October peak of $136 a barrel. In the Strait of Hormuz, Iran's Revolutionary Guards claimed two more tankers hit mines, bringing their claimed total to six sunk in two days, but there is still no independent confirmation; the UK's maritime agency separately confirmed a tanker was hit by an unknown projectile off Oman on Monday. Saudi Arabia's East-West oil pipeline was attacked again on Monday, with operations unaffected, while Houthi fighters advanced to within about 100 km of Aden. Goldman Sachs expects a 2 million bpd global surplus in 2027, with Brent averaging $88 a barrel.

UK maritime agency confirms two separate attacks: tanker hit off Oman, ADNOC tanker heads back to Dubai.** The UK Maritime Trade Operations centre said on Monday that a tanker leaving the strait was struck by an unknown projectile, causing an engine-room fire, with all crew safe and no casualties; maritime security firm Vanguard identified the vessel as the Panama-flagged On Phoenix, struck about three nautical miles off the coast of Oman. Hormuz traffic still only about 8% of normal; just 6 crossings by Monday evening.** As of 19:23 UTC on November 16, only 6 commercial ships had crossed the strait that day (1 inbound, 5 outbound, including 2 tankers); the 7-day average is 7.1 a day, about 8% of the pre-crisis average of 84.8 a day. **5. Saudi East-West pipeline attacked again Monday, operations unaffected; Houthis advance toward Aden.** A Saudi energy ministry official said the East-West pipeline was attacked in the Medina region on Monday, causing a limited fire that was extinguished, with no injuries and operations unaffected; officials did not name the attacker. The Houthis had earlier claimed they hit Riyadh airport and a financial centre with missiles on Sunday and struck pumping stations No. 5 and No. 6 with drones, while the coalition said it intercepted missiles aimed at Riyadh and drones in several regions. Separately, Reuters cited Yemeni military sources as saying Houthi fighters have advanced to within about 100 km of Aden, the temporary capital, after government forces withdrew from positions near the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, raising the risk to Red Sea shipping. The pipeline now carries about 4 million barrels a day to Yanbu on the Red Sea, roughly 4% of global supply.
