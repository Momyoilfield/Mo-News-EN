# Mo Daily News · Sunday, November 15, 2026

## Daily Oil Overview

Oil markets were closed on Saturday, leaving prices at Friday's settles: Brent crude at $95.84 a barrel and US WTI at $88.56. Both benchmarks lost 2.5% on the week, their second consecutive weekly decline. The Strait of Hormuz saw three more tankers reported hit by mines on Saturday; Iran said the vessels had switched off their tracking systems and were using "unauthorised routes", but named no ships and gave no casualty figures, and there was no independent confirmation — commercial traffic through the strait is now about 7% of pre-war levels. In Washington, President Trump said on Thursday that the US "doesn't need a deal with Iran" and moved Vice President Vance off the negotiations; Secretary of State Marco Rubio said on Friday the war is "militarily effectively over" and economic pressure is enough. Iran's seven-day roadmap to end the war is still awaiting a US reply, and The Wall Street Journal reported on Friday that Trump has rejected it.

## News in Brief

**1. Markets closed Saturday; oil flat at Friday's settles, Brent $95.84.** Futures markets were closed on Saturday, so prices stayed at Friday's settles: Brent crude at $95.84 a barrel, up 2 cents, and WTI at $88.56, down 0.2%. Both benchmarks lost 2.5% on the week, their second weekly decline in a row; last week Brent plunged 16% and WTI 13%. The EIA expects Brent to stay near $100 a barrel through the end of the year in its November outlook; futures reopen on Sunday evening.

**2. Strait of Hormuz: Iran says three tankers hit mines on Saturday; vessels unnamed, no independent confirmation.** Iran's Revolutionary Guards navy said on Saturday that two tankers struck mines and caught fire in the southern strait, one of them a very large crude carrier, claiming the vessels had switched off their radar and positioning systems and were using an "unauthorised route"; Iran's navy separately said a third tanker hit a mine east of the strait and caught fire. No names, flags, cargoes or casualty figures were given, and Reuters said it could not immediately verify the reports. Including Saturday's three, at least 14 ships have been attacked in November; commercial traffic through the strait is about 7% of pre-war levels, averaging 6.3 crossings a day over the past week against 84.8 a day before the crisis.

**3. Trump: the US "doesn't need a deal with Iran"; Vance moved to domestic issues.** President Trump wrote on Truth Social on Thursday: "We don't need a deal with Iran... We've got their money, Hundreds of Billions of Dollars, and we've got their Strait!" He also told Vice President Vance, who had been leading the negotiations, to focus on domestic issues instead. In an interview on Thursday, Trump added that he would order new air strikes "without hesitation" if things went the other way, and said it would be a "defeat" if Iran's current leadership were still in power when he leaves office.

**4. Iran's seven-day roadmap still awaits a US reply; The Wall Street Journal says Trump has rejected it.** Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said on Friday outside the UN Security Council that Tehran has submitted a seven-day roadmap to Washington through Qatar: the US would lift its naval blockade of Iranian ports, ease sanctions on Iranian oil sales, release about $12 billion in frozen Iranian assets and observe a ceasefire covering Lebanon and Yemen, while Iran would reopen the Strait of Hormuz on day six and start nuclear talks on day seven. The Wall Street Journal reported on Friday that Trump has rejected the plan; US officials said he privately doubts Iran would meet his demands. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said on Friday that the war is "militarily effectively over" and that economic pressure makes new large-scale military action unnecessary.

**5. Iran seizes a tanker; US disables two Iranian tankers.** Iran's Revolutionary Guards said on Friday that they had seized the tanker Ocean Koi in the Gulf of Oman, accusing it of trying to "disrupt oil exports and the interests of the Iranian nation"; the vessel is registered in Barbados, and state television aired footage of the boarding. US Central Command said the same day that US forces had disabled two Iranian-flagged tankers as they tried to enter Iranian ports in the Gulf of Oman. A day earlier, the US and Iran had traded fire in the Strait of Hormuz: Trump said Iran attacked three US Navy destroyers, while Iran said the US struck an Iranian tanker and another vessel, wounding 10 sailors with five more missing.

**6. Three agencies split in November outlooks: OPEC sees demand edging up, EIA and IEA see it falling.** OPEC's November report expects world oil demand of about 105.7 million barrels a day in 2026, modest growth of around 0.2 million a day, unchanged from its October forecast; the EIA expects demand to fall by 2.0 million barrels a day, and the IEA by 2.7 million, to 102.3 million. All three expect a rebound of about 2 million barrels a day in 2027. The EIA expects Brent to stay near $100 a barrel through the end of the year, averaging $97.44 in 2026 and $85.78 in 2027. OPEC+ next meets on November 29.
