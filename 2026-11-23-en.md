# Mo Daily News · Monday, November 23, 2026

## Daily Oil Overview

Markets are closed for the weekend, so prices stand at Friday's settlement: Brent for January settled at $92.57 a barrel and WTI for December at $83.98. For the week, Brent fell 3.4% and WTI fell 5.2%, a third straight weekly decline, and Brent is down about 20% so far in November. The Gulf situation escalated further over the weekend: a CNN analysis of United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO) data shows attacks on shipping near the Strait of Hormuz are at their highest level since the war began, with more than 30 in October and at least 22 in November as of Sunday; three tankers were hit and caught fire on Friday and Saturday, Iran said on Sunday that two supertankers had hit mines, and another tanker incident involving military forces was reported in the Gulf of Oman on Sunday evening. Iran also fired missiles and drones at a civilian industrial facility in Bahrain on Saturday night, wounding two people, and Bahrain has asked the UN Security Council to act and is seeking compensation. The US-Iran deadlock shows no sign of easing: President Trump says the US "doesn't need a deal" with Iran, while Iran's foreign minister says reopening the strait is out of the question until Washington accepts Tehran's conditions; meanwhile the US blockade of Iranian ports could drive Iran's oil revenues to zero by mid-December. On demand, China's crude imports from the Gulf jumped 42.4% month on month in October, but refinery throughput fell and inventories surged, pointing to stockpiling rather than stronger consumption. This week: futures reopen on Monday evening, US markets close for Thanksgiving on Thursday, and OPEC+ meets on November 29.

**2. Hormuz attacks at their highest since the war began: more than 30 in October, at least 22 in November as of Sunday; three tankers hit and set ablaze on Friday and Saturday.** A CNN analysis of UKMTO data shows that reported attacks in and around the Strait of Hormuz, the Persian Gulf and the Gulf of Oman exceeded 30 in October, the first time above 30 since the US-Iran war began in late February; as of Sunday, at least 22 had been reported in November, the second-highest figure since the war began, against an average of around 10 a month before October. Maritime risk firm Marisks said three tankers leaving the strait were struck on Friday and Saturday: the Captain Leon, a Marshall Islands-flagged chemical and oil tanker, was hit in the bow and caught fire at 3 p.m. UTC on Friday; the Torkel, a Hong Kong-flagged crude tanker, caught fire after being hit in the stern at 5 a.m. UTC on Saturday; and less than an hour later the Arina, also Hong Kong-flagged, was struck in the engine room by a missile. All crews were reported safe. Marisks did not confirm who was behind the attacks, though it said Iranian involvement was "assessed as likely." Data from the International Maritime Organization also points to October as the month with the most confirmed incidents so far this year.

**3. Iran says two supertankers hit mines and caught fire; another tanker incident involving military forces reported in the Gulf of Oman on Sunday evening, possibly linked to the US blockade line.** Iran's Army Navy said on Sunday that two supertankers exploded and were immobilised after striking naval mines in a "restricted area" east of the Strait of Hormuz, north of Ghanam Island, accusing them of switching off their identification and navigation systems and entering the zone in violation of the rules; the claim has not been independently verified, and it is unclear whether it is connected to Saturday's two attacks. On Sunday evening, UKMTO reported that a tanker was involved in an incident "with military forces" in the Gulf of Oman, near the southern Iranian city of Chabahar, close to the US Navy's blockade line and consistent with where an Iranian tanker would be if it had sailed from Kharg Island on Sunday morning to break the blockade; UKMTO said only that "authorities are aware and investigations are ongoing." More than 14 Iranian tankers were reported to be gathering off Kharg Island to challenge the US blockade, but no departure had been confirmed as of Sunday morning; Kpler estimated about 43 million barrels of Iranian oil stranded inside the blockade zone.

**4. Iranian missiles strike a civilian industrial facility in Bahrain: fire and two wounded; Bahrain seeks UN Security Council action and compensation.** Bahrain's Interior Ministry said an Iranian attack late on Saturday targeted a civilian industrial facility, causing a fire and material damage; on Sunday it reported two people wounded, a Bahraini national with a disability who is in stable condition and a Pakistani resident who is in critical condition. Residents saw smoke rising over Sitra Island, which hosts several industrial facilities including a refinery run by the state oil company Bapco, though Bahraini officials did not identify the site hit. Iran's Revolutionary Guards said instead that they had targeted US Patriot air defence systems at the al-Riffa and Awali bases in Bahrain and claimed to have destroyed two radars, a claim that has not been independently confirmed. Bahrain's Foreign Ministry on Sunday demanded action by the UN Security Council and compensation from Iran, saying the facility hit was purely civilian and industrial; Saudi Arabia condemned the attack as "criminal." It is the first time Iranian strikes on Bahrain, which resumed on November 13, have wounded civilians.

**6. China's Gulf crude imports jumped 42.4% month on month in October, the biggest monthly surge since the war began — but refinery runs fell, so it is stockpiling, not stronger demand.** Chinese customs data shows China's crude imports from the Persian Gulf rose 42.4% in October versus September, the largest single-month increase since the Iran war began reshaping regional trade. At the same time, China's October refinery throughput fell 2% month on month and 8% year on year to 13.79 million barrels a day, while implied crude inventories grew by 492,000 barrels a day, the largest build since March — showing China is buying cheap oil to stockpile, not because consumption is strengthening. Analysts note that China is both buying heavily to refine and resell at attractive crack spreads and building stocks, a pattern that reinforces the 2027 surplus narrative and matches the IEA's view of a 1.7 million barrel-a-day surplus in the fourth quarter.
