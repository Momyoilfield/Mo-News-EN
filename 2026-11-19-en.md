## Daily Oil Overview

Oil prices fell sharply on Wednesday: US Energy Information Administration (EIA) data showed US crude and fuel inventories rising far more than expected, and expectations of a supply surplus outweighed fears of escalation in the Middle East. On Thursday, Trump, Xi and Putin will hold their first trilateral meeting in Shenzhen, with nuclear arms control and the Iran war on the agenda.

**2. EIA: US commercial crude stocks up 3.4 million barrels to 417.8 million in the week ended November 13; gasoline up 5.2 million, distillates up 1.6 million — all above expectations.** The EIA said on Wednesday that US commercial crude inventories rose by 3.4 million barrels in the week ended November 13, to 417.8 million barrels, against a Reuters poll expectation of a rise of just 58,000 barrels; gasoline stocks rose by 5.2 million barrels to 215.9 million, against expectations of a 1.6 million-barrel build; distillate stocks rose by 1.6 million barrels to 104.9 million, against expectations of a 1.3 million-barrel rise. The industry group API had estimated on Tuesday a crude build of 5.28 million barrels and a gasoline build of 5.12 million.

**5. Iraq restarts Kirkuk-Ceyhan oil pipeline at 120,000 bpd, targeting 500,000 bpd.** Iraq's Oil Minister said on Tuesday that crude exports through the Kirkuk-Ceyhan pipeline to Türkiye have resumed, starting at 120,000 barrels a day and rising gradually to 500,000 bpd. The 960-km pipeline links Iraq's northern oilfields with the Turkish Mediterranean port of Ceyhan and had been shut for 12 years since attacks by Islamic State militants in 2014; Iraq's cabinet approved rehabilitating the pipeline on the same day. The route gives Iraq an additional export outlet bypassing the Strait of Hormuz, though it is not yet clear whether the volumes are extra supply or barrels diverted from existing routes.
