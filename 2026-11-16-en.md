# Mo Daily News · Monday, November 16, 2026

## Daily Oil Overview

Oil markets were closed on Sunday, leaving prices at Friday's settles: Brent crude at $95.84 a barrel and US WTI at $88.56. Both benchmarks lost 2.5% on the week, their second consecutive weekly decline. The weekend's biggest story was a claim by Iran's Revolutionary Guards on Sunday that a very large crude carrier carrying more than 2 million barrels of oil sank after hitting a mine — but no ship was named and there was no independent confirmation; the UK's maritime agency separately reported a tanker attacked in Gulf waters, with all crew safe. Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia escalated, with the Saudi-led coalition saying on Sunday it had intercepted missiles aimed at Riyadh and drones over the Eastern Province, and Gulf stock markets fell. US officials said President Trump has no appetite for restarting the war with Iran for now. Commercial traffic through the Strait of Hormuz is still only about 8% of pre-war levels.

**2. Revolutionary Guards say a supertanker carrying over 2 million barrels sank after a mine strike; no independent confirmation.** Iran's Revolutionary Guards said on Sunday afternoon that two tankers struck mines and caught fire south of the Strait of Hormuz, one of them a very large crude carrier carrying more than 2 million barrels of oil, which had sunk, while the other was still burning. The statement named no ship, flag, owner or position and carried no images; by Sunday evening, no shipowner, flag state or navy had confirmed a sinking. The UK Maritime Trade Operations centre separately reported that, according to Indian shipping authorities, a tanker was attacked while sailing east through Gulf waters before entering the strait, with all crew safe and the extent of damage unknown; the centre said it was unclear whether this was the incident the Guards had announced.

**3. Hormuz traffic still only about 8% of normal; just 4 crossings by Sunday evening.** As of 19:17 UTC on Sunday, only 4 commercial ships had crossed the strait that day (2 inbound, 2 outbound, including 2 tankers); the 7-day average is 6.4 a day, about 8% of the pre-crisis average of 84.8 a day. Over the past 7 days there were 45 crossings in total, including 14 tankers and no LNG or LPG carriers.

**4. Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia escalate; coalition intercepts missiles aimed at Riyadh; Gulf bourses fall.** The Saudi-led coalition said on Sunday it had shot down two ballistic missiles fired at the capital, Riyadh, and three drones over the oil-rich Eastern Province. Houthi strikes on Saudi airports, seaports and energy installations have intensified, and Riyadh has expanded its air campaign against Houthi targets in Yemen. Gulf stock markets closed lower on Sunday on the Hormuz disruption and the Houthi attacks: Saudi Arabia's benchmark index fell 0.7% and Qatar's 1.8%. Separately, Poland's Orlen has cancelled a long-term LNG contract with Qatar, citing the force majeure Qatar declared after an Iranian attack in March — the first such cancellation since the conflict began, which analysts say could save Orlen up to $2 billion.

**5. Trump has no appetite for restarting the Iran war for now; he has told Congress the war is "terminated".** A major US newspaper reported on Friday, citing a US official, that President Trump has no appetite for reigniting the war with Iran "for now", and that the administration has settled on a middle course of no new war, no negotiations and no lasting peace — keeping the status quo. Trump wrote to Congress last week saying US hostilities with Iran have "terminated", citing a ceasefire that began on April 7; the Senate rejected an Iran War Powers resolution for a sixth time on Thursday. Iran's seven-day roadmap to end the war is still awaiting a US reply.

US commercial crude stocks rose by almost 5 million barrels.** US Energy Information Administration data showed commercial crude oil stocks, excluding the Strategic Petroleum Reserve, rose by almost 5 million barrels in the week to November 6, to 414.4 million barrels, with gasoline and distillate stocks also higher. The bigger-than-expected build, together with weaker refined-product demand, offset some of the geopolitical support for prices.
