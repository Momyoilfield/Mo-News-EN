# Mo Daily News · Wednesday, November 11, 2026

## Today's Oil Headlines

Oil prices fell again on Tuesday: Brent closed at $91.49 a barrel, down 1.2%, after briefly dipping below $90 during the session for the first time since August 31; US WTI closed at $86.04, down 0.9%. Brent has fallen about 7% since last Friday as the market keeps pricing in an easing of the US–Iran standoff. But the US Energy Information Administration (EIA) raised its price forecasts the same day, expecting Brent to average $105 a barrel in the fourth quarter — the official view and the market's direction are pointing opposite ways for now. China's crude imports rebounded sharply in October, while its fuel exports fell. US October inflation came in hotter than expected, raising the odds of a December rate hike. OPEC releases its monthly report today, and the International Energy Agency follows on Friday.

EIA raised its oil price forecast: Brent to average $97 this year and $105 in Q4.** The US Energy Information Administration's November Short-Term Energy Outlook expects Brent to average $97 a barrel in 2026 and fall back to $86 in 2027; the fourth-quarter average of $105 is $14 higher than last month's forecast. US crude output will reach 13.9 million barrels a day this year — a fourth straight annual record — and rise further to 14.3 million in 2027. Qatar: both the US and Iran responded positively to mediation, but no deal yet.** Qatar's Foreign Ministry said on Tuesday that Washington and Tehran had both given positive responses to Qatari mediation, which continues with the aim of ending the "no war, no peace" deadlock. Trump said the war with Iran was "militarily effectively over", while keeping the option of fresh strikes open. Iran has compressed its ceasefire plan from a seven-day timeline to two days, but still insists on seven preconditions, including lifting the blockade, releasing frozen assets and easing sanctions; Tehran denied reports of a deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz in exchange for $8 billion in unfrozen funds.

**4. Saudi East-West pipeline attacked again; operations unaffected.** Saudi Arabia's Energy Ministry said the East-West pipeline was attacked in the Medina region on November 9, causing a limited fire that was extinguished, with no injuries and no effect on operations; Riyadh did not name the attacker. The 1,200 km pipeline carries about 4 million barrels of crude a day around the Strait of Hormuz, roughly 4% of global supply. The same day, the Saudi-led coalition said it had intercepted two Houthi missiles fired toward Riyadh.

**5. China's October crude imports rebounded to 9.88 million barrels a day, a seven-month high.** Chinese customs data released on Tuesday showed October crude imports of 9.88 million barrels a day, up 9.4% from September and the highest since March, though still 13.3% below a year earlier; imports for the first ten months of the year are down 15.1% year on year. Crude arrivals from the Middle East recovered to 4.65 million barrels a day, back to pre-war levels. Fuel exports fell from 7.16 million tons in September to 5.18 million tons, a sign China is keeping more crude for its domestic refiners.

Markets now see a 25-basis-point Federal Reserve hike in December as largely locked in; falling oil prices are one of the few bright spots in the inflation picture.
