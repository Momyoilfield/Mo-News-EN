# Mo Daily News · Sunday, November 22, 2026

## Daily Oil Overview

Markets are closed for the weekend, so prices stand at Friday's settlement: Brent for January settled at $92.57 a barrel and WTI for December at $83.98. For the week, Brent fell 3.4% and WTI fell 5.2%, a third straight weekly decline, and Brent is down about 20% so far in November. On Saturday, two more tankers were attacked in the Strait of Hormuz; Iran said one tanker caught fire after hitting a mine and claimed to have shot down a US MQ-9 drone. President Trump said a decision on a new round of strikes on Iran will wait until early next year, easing the near-term risk of a major war, although two US carrier groups are already in the region. Supply continues to weigh on prices: the International Energy Agency flipped its fourth-quarter balance from a deficit of 1.8 million barrels a day to a surplus of 1.7 million, the US rig count rose to its highest since April 2024 and US crude output set a fourth straight weekly record; OPEC+ meets on November 29 to decide the 2027 production framework.

**2. Two more tankers hit in the Strait of Hormuz on Saturday — one in the stern, one in the engine room; Iran says a tanker hit a mine and caught fire, and claims it shot down a US MQ-9 drone.** The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO) agency said on Saturday that an outbound tanker was struck in the stern by an unknown projectile about 11 nautical miles north of Khasab, Oman, and that another tanker was hit in its engine room about 17 nautical miles east of Khasab; there were no casualties in either incident, and it was unclear whether the same vessel was involved in both. Iran's navy said afterwards that a tanker had caught fire after striking a mine in a restricted area east of the strait, accusing it of switching off its identification and navigation systems and entering the zone in violation of the rules — a claim that has not been independently verified. Iran's Revolutionary Guards said on the same day that its air defences had shot down a US MQ-9 drone over the strait, the 58th such drone it claims to have downed; the US has not confirmed this. Al Jazeera reported about 25 incidents near the strait in the past week, roughly three a day; since the war began at the end of February, more than 120 commercial vessels have been attacked in and around the strait, killing at least 24 seafarers, and October's 28 attacks were the highest monthly toll.

**3. Trump: decision on new Iran strikes waits until early next year; Netanyahu presses for a four- to seven-day joint air campaign, and two US carrier groups are in place.** President Trump told Axios aboard Air Force One on Thursday, returning from China: "I am not rushing into any decision… We will see what happens after the beginning of the year." He told Reuters "we've got time" and told Fox News "we'll see what happens after January 1." Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu is pressing Washington to join a four- to seven-day air campaign; Israeli officials have called an operation "unavoidable," and Israel's ambassador to the US said Israel could act alone if necessary. Two US carrier groups are deployed in the region and could launch strikes within two to four days of an order, targeting Iran's missile and drone arsenal, energy facilities and Revolutionary Guard command posts; the White House has asked the Pentagon to assess the risk of Iranian retaliation against Gulf oil and gas infrastructure. Divisions inside the administration remain deep: Secretary of State Marco Rubio argues the blockade and sanctions are already working and no major combat is needed, while Qatar, Saudi Arabia and the UAE have urged a pause to allow negotiations.

**4. IEA: fourth-quarter balance flips from deficit to a 1.7 million b/d surplus; EIA expects stocks to build by 3.5 million b/d in the first half of 2027.** The International Energy Agency's November report said global oil supply rose by 2.3 million barrels a day in October to 105.4 million barrels a day, and flipped its fourth-quarter balance from a previously forecast deficit of 1.8 million barrels a day to a surplus of 1.7 million; the IEA also expects a supply overhang of 3.8 million barrels a day in 2027. US Energy Information Administration data showed US crude stocks rose by 3.4 million barrels in the week to November 13, to 417.8 million barrels, and the EIA expects global stocks to build by an average of 3.5 million barrels a day in the first half of 2027. Surplus expectations are the main drag on prices this round, with Brent well below its October levels above $110 a barrel.

**5. US rig count rises to 607, highest since April 2024; crude output sets a fourth straight weekly record at 14.032 million b/d.** Baker Hughes reported on Friday that the total US oil and gas rig count rose by six to 607 in the week to November 20, its highest since April 26, 2024, and up 53 from a year earlier; oil rigs rose by six to 469, their highest since May 2025, gas rigs rose by one to 128, and oil rigs in the Permian Basin rose by two to 277. EIA data showed US crude production rose to 14.032 million barrels a day in the week to November 13, a fourth consecutive weekly record, up 198,000 barrels a day from a year earlier.

**6. OPEC+ meets on November 29 to decide the 2027 production framework; analysts see Brent averaging $86–$88 in 2027.** The 42nd OPEC+ ministerial meeting is set for Sunday, November 29, when it will decide the 2027 production framework and new baselines for each member; a layer of cuts of about 2 million barrels a day runs through the end of 2026, and the capacity review was due to be completed in mid-November. Forecasts for 2027: Goldman Sachs sees Brent averaging $88 a barrel and falling to $78 by year-end; Morgan Stanley sees $86.30; the EIA sees an average of about $86. Analysts say the November 29 decision will be pivotal — if disrupted Gulf barrels return in large numbers, the 2027 surplus could deepen.
