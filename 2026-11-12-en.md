# Mo Daily News · Thursday, November 12, 2026

## Daily Oil Overview

Oil prices rebounded on Wednesday: Brent crude closed at $92.76 a barrel, up 1.4%, and US WTI closed at $86.53, up 0.6%, both pulling away from the two-month lows hit on Tuesday. The rebound was driven by a tough statement from Iran — Tehran said it would not let "a single atom" of enriched uranium leave the country and denied it is negotiating with the United States, cooling hopes for a peace deal. But supply keeps recovering fast: OPEC's monthly report showed OPEC+ output jumped by 2.04 million barrels a day in October, with Saudi output back up to 10.56 million barrels a day, the highest since the war began, while Middle East crude outages have fallen to about 3.8 million barrels a day. The Strait of Hormuz is still troubled waters — 37 ships were attacked there in October, the highest monthly total since the war began. Official US inventory data comes out on Thursday, and the IEA's monthly report follows on Friday.

OPEC report: OPEC+ output jumped 2.04 million bpd in October; Saudi back to 10.56 million bpd.** OPEC's November monthly report showed total OPEC+ crude output reached 41.23 million barrels a day in October, up 2.04 million from September. Strait of Hormuz: 37 ships attacked in October, the highest monthly total since the war began.** Data from the Washington Institute for Near East Policy showed 37 ships were attacked in the Strait of Hormuz and nearby shipping lanes in October, the highest monthly total since the war began. Iran's Revolutionary Guards Navy said on Tuesday that two tankers caught fire after hitting naval mines on an "unauthorized route" in the strait. Analysts say that even if a political deal is reached, shipping is unlikely to return to normal soon — it would take mine clearance, lower insurance costs, and shipowners willing to take the risk.

**5. US crude stocks rose about 9 million barrels; strategic reserve seen hitting a new low.** Data from the American Petroleum Institute showed US crude inventories rose by about 9 million barrels last week; distillate stocks such as diesel rose by 1.5 million barrels, after falling below 100 million barrels for the first time since 2003. Official EIA inventory data was delayed to Thursday noon by the Veterans Day holiday. The EIA also expects the US Strategic Petroleum Reserve to fall to a new low of about 240 million barrels in the first half of next year, and raised its 2027 forecast for average US retail gasoline prices to $3.73 a gallon.
