**1. Oil up for a third day on Thursday: Brent settles at $96.57, up $1.96 (2.1%); WTI has no settlement on Thanksgiving and is quoted at $84.84, up 1.4%.** Brent for January settled on Thursday at $96.57 a barrel, up $1.96 (2.1%), its third consecutive daily gain, and is now up 4.3% on the week from last Friday's close of $92.57; WTI had no settlement because of the Thanksgiving holiday and was up $1.16 (1.39%) at $84.84 as of 1:21 p.m. ET. Analysts say slowing flows through the Strait of Hormuz, low inventories and "known, acute risk" to about 15% of global supply make the outlook for crude prices bullish; another analyst said Brent had been oversold when it dipped below $90 and a rebound was only a matter of time.

**2. Hormuz: the Revolutionary Guards say two more tankers hit mines and caught fire, one a supertanker carrying more than 2 million barrels of crude; an LNG carrier exploded after hitting a mine.** Iran's Revolutionary Guards said on Thursday that two tankers exploded and caught fire after hitting sea mines north of Oman's Khasab port, because they had taken a route Tehran does not recognise; one of the vessels was a supertanker carrying more than 2 million barrels of crude oil. The IRGC navy also said an LNG carrier exploded and caught fire after striking a naval mine while trying to use a route Iran had not authorised. None of these claims has been independently verified, and no vessel names, crew details or damage assessments have been released. The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations agency reported that on November 25 a tanker encountered an explosion from an unidentified projectile about 50 nautical miles west of Dubai, with the crew safe and no damage reported. Eight major shipping associations issued a joint open letter on November 26 calling for concrete measures to safeguard freedom of navigation and protect seafarers. US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said on Wednesday that since Washington launched Operation Economic Outcast in August, Iran has "failed to load a single tanker" and "doesn't have a drop of oil on the water left to sell."

**3. Houthi attacks spread to the Jazan refinery: one worker killed, several injured, partial shutdown; loadings at Ras Tanura suspended.** A projectile struck a refinery in Saudi Arabia's southwestern Jazan region on Thursday, killing a Nepalese worker and injuring several others, according to the Saudi Ministry of Energy; the strike sparked a fire and temporarily suspended some operations at the facility. It came a day after the Houthis said they had attacked Saudi Arabia's largest refinery at Ras Tanura, with satellite images showing thick black smoke over the plant; a shipping source told CNN on Thursday that oil loadings at the Ras Tanura terminal were suspended after the attack, and it is unclear whether they have resumed. Saudi Arabia separately reported that oil export revenues in September rose 25.6% year on year to 87.1 billion riyals ($23.2 billion), with petroleum accounting for 75.4% of merchandise exports that month.

**4. OPEC+: sources say Sunday's meeting may roll over current output targets at least into the first quarter of 2027.** Reuters reports, citing two people with knowledge of the talks, that OPEC+ is likely to maintain its current oil output targets at Sunday's meeting for at least the first quarter of 2027, as disagreements persist over the capacity review and the Iran war continues to disrupt Middle East exports; no decision has yet been reached and talks continue. OPEC+ ministers will begin a series of four meetings on Sunday at 1300 GMT to decide 2027 output policy, and production cuts of about 2 million barrels a day expire at the end of the year. Rystad Energy expects a global supply surplus averaging around 5 million barrels a day next year, even with only a partial recovery in Gulf flows.

**5. Venezuela: Eni and Repsol pledge to jointly invest $2 billion over five years in their joint ventures with PDVSA.** Italy's Eni and Spain's Repsol have "committed to jointly investing" $2 billion over five years in their joint ventures with Venezuelan state energy firm PDVSA, according to a macroeconomic framework published by the Venezuelan government on Tuesday. The two companies have also reached agreements to more than double offshore gas production at the Cardón IV field in the Gulf of Venezuela and export the fuel as liquefied natural gas via a floating terminal, targeting the start of exports by the end of 2031. The two companies have operated in Venezuela for decades but have not published detailed investment plans.

A drone attack at the Slavyansk ECO oil refinery in Russia's southern Krasnodar region also led to a fire, inflicting serious damage.
