## News in Brief

**1. Oil falls sharply on Monday: Brent settles at $89.37, down 3.5%; WTI at $80.17, down 3.9% — both their lowest since August.** Brent for January settled on Monday at $89.37 a barrel, down $3.20 (3.5%), its lowest close since August 28; WTI for January settled at $80.17, down $3.24 (3.9%), its lowest since August 7. The main driver was recovering supply outweighing attack risk: Middle East crude exports are flowing again and Libya's Sharara pipeline reopened, alongside risk-off sentiment from a selloff in US equities. Kpler data shows crude flows through the Strait of Hormuz averaged about 9.5 million barrels a day in the 10 days to November 19, down from 12.2 million in October, but still moving. The IEA's November Oil Market Report said global oil supply has exceeded demand for the first time since the Iran war began, with a fourth-quarter surplus of about 1.7 million barrels a day; JPMorgan expects persistent oversupply to hold Brent in the high $70s in the first half of 2027.

**2. Libya's largest oilfield pipeline at Sharara reopens after a shutdown of about 10 hours; the 350,000 bpd output threat is lifted, while the Hamada line stays shut.** Libya's National Oil Corporation (NOC) said an "outlaw group" closed a valve on the pipeline from the Sharara field to the coastal port of Zawiya on Sunday, threatening about 350,000 barrels a day of output; after a shutdown of nearly 10 hours, pumping resumed on Monday and crude began flowing into storage tanks at the Akakus facility in Zawiya, NOC chairman Masoud Suleiman confirmed to media. Sharara is Libya's largest oilfield, and this was the third valve incident in the country's western export system since September. A second pipeline, from the Hamada field to the Zawiya refinery, has been closed since October 30 and remains shut, costing about 15,000 barrels a day.

**3. Hormuz attacks at their highest since the war began: maritime body rates the threat "severe" and outbound tanker traffic is down about 30%.** A CNN analysis of United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO) data shows reported attacks in and around the Strait of Hormuz exceeded 30 in October and reached at least 22 in November as of Sunday, both the highest levels since the war began; the Joint Maritime Information Center (JMIC) rated the threat level for the strait "severe" in its November 22 assessment. Outbound tanker traffic has shrunk visibly: weekly transits fell from 52 vessels to 37 in mid-November, a drop of nearly 29%. Iranian lawmaker Abolfazl Abutorabi claimed on November 23 that Iran's armed forces intercept about 10 "violating" large tankers a day; Iran's military has not formally acknowledged missile or drone strikes on tankers, and its official account remains that tankers hit mines. Three tankers were struck and caught fire over the weekend, with all crews reported safe.

**4. Trump warns Iranian tankers that try to run the blockade will end up "at the bottom of the sea" and strikes could resume after the New Year; Iran says it will use "all available means" to break the blockade.** Responding to reports that more than 14 Iranian tankers have gathered off Kharg Island to challenge the US blockade, President Trump told Fox News on November 22: "I hope they do; they'll all be at the bottom of the sea like the rest of the navy." He also said he was inclined to resume military strikes on Iran after the New Year if Washington and Tehran fail to reach an agreement by then. Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei said on November 23 that Iran would use "all available means" against the blockade; a deputy foreign minister had already called the blockade an "act of war" on Saturday. A proposal in IRGC-linked media, dubbed "Operation Fereydoun," calls for sending a tanker convoy through the blockade and threatening strikes on Gulf energy infrastructure if Washington intervenes, but no official in Tehran has formally confirmed such a move. Tanker-tracking firm TankerTrackers said the widely shared track of the Iranian tanker Khat Shekan supposedly breaching the blockade was a spoofed signal, with nothing on satellite imagery to corroborate it.

**5. US gasoline keeps getting cheaper: national average about $3.64 a gallon, though Thanksgiving travel could still set a holiday record.** AAA data puts the US national average for regular gasoline at $3.6366 a gallon on November 23, down from $3.8118 a week earlier and $4.2926 a month earlier; falling crude prices, cheaper winter-blend fuel and weaker seasonal demand are all pushing prices down. GasBuddy expects prices to average about $3.62 on Thanksgiving Day, which would still beat the previous holiday record of $3.56 set in 2022. US gasoline inventories rose 5.1 million barrels last week to 215.9 million barrels. Analysts say prices could fall further before Thursday if crude keeps sliding, but with attacks on Middle East tanker traffic continuing and supply risks unresolved, it is not certain the record will be avoided.
