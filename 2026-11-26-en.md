# Mo Daily News · Thursday, November 26, 2026

## Daily Oil Overview

Oil surged for a second straight day on Wednesday: Brent for January settled at $94.61 a barrel, up 3.5%; WTI for January settled at $83.68, up 2%, with Brent at its highest in over a week. The rally was driven by a fresh escalation in the Middle East: Yemen's Houthis said they attacked Saudi Arabia's largest refinery at Ras Tanura, with satellite images showing thick smoke over the plant, and more tankers reportedly hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz. Iran also named its price for reopening the strait — if Washington lifts its naval blockade of Iranian ports and ends the war, Tehran says it can restore normal shipping within 48 hours. The weekly EIA report, which showed a smaller-than-expected US inventory build, also supported prices. But supply remains ample: US commercial crude stocks rose for a third straight week, and US crude output hit a record 14.065 million barrels a day. OPEC+ meets on Sunday, November 29, to decide its 2027 production framework.

## News in Brief

**1. Oil up for a second day on Wednesday: Brent settles at $94.61, up 3.5%; WTI at $83.68, up 2%, its highest in over a week.** Brent for January settled on Wednesday at $94.61 a barrel, up $3.21 (3.51%); WTI for January settled at $83.68, up $1.63 (1.99%). Both benchmarks gave up early losses and climbed through the day on the Houthi attack on a Saudi refinery and fresh tanker incidents in the Strait of Hormuz, with Brent closing at its highest in over a week and the Brent–WTI spread widening to $10.93. Despite two days of gains, Brent is still about 20% below its late-October peak of around $118, and worries about a supply surplus have not gone away.

**2. Hormuz: Iran says two more tankers hit mines and caught fire, with an LNG carrier and a Hong Kong-flagged tanker also hit; Tehran's price — lift the port blockade, end the war, and shipping reopens in 48 hours.** Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps navy said two tankers exploded and caught fire early Wednesday after striking mines in the southern and eastern waters of the Strait of Hormuz, because they had taken a route Tehran does not recognise; Iran's military later said a third tanker hit a mine in a "forbidden zone", and the IRGC navy also said an LNG carrier exploded after hitting a mine. None of these claims has been independently verified, and no vessel names, crew details or damage assessments have been released. Maritime security firm Ambrey confirmed that a Hong Kong-flagged tanker was hit by an unidentified projectile about 9.5 nautical miles off Lima, Oman, on Wednesday, sustaining minimal damage and continuing its transit, with no casualties. Through Qatari mediators, Iran told Washington that if the US lifts its naval blockade of Iranian ports and ends the war on all fronts, Iran can restore normal shipping through the strait within 48 hours. The US Virtual Embassy in Iran urged American citizens in the country to leave at once on the same day.

**3. Houthis attack Saudi Arabia's largest refinery at Ras Tanura: Riyadh says it shot down four drones, while satellite images show thick smoke over the plant.** Yemen's Houthis said on Wednesday they attacked the Ras Tanura refinery — Saudi Arabia's largest, processing 550,000 barrels a day — and that it caused fires; they also claimed attacks on the King Abdullah Financial District in Riyadh and the ports of Jeddah and Jizan. The Saudi-led coalition said air defences destroyed four drones over Ras Tanura on Wednesday morning, with fragments falling and no injuries in an initial assessment, but did not say the refinery had been hit. Satellite imagery from both NASA and Planet Labs showed thick black smoke over the installation, and a source with knowledge of the matter confirmed to Reuters that the refinery was attacked. Saudi Arabia separately confirmed that Jizan Port was struck by an unidentified projectile, causing minor damage. This is the first refinery attack claimed by the Houthis this autumn to be corroborated by satellite imagery.

**4. US inventories rise for a third straight week, but by less than expected; US crude output hits a record 14.065 million barrels a day.** EIA data show US commercial crude inventories rose by 1.1 million barrels to 418.9 million in the week ending November 20, a third consecutive weekly build, but smaller than the roughly 2 million barrel increase the market expected, and still about 3% below the five-year average; gasoline stocks rose 1.7 million barrels to 217.7 million and distillate stocks rose 715,000 barrels to 105.6 million. Refinery utilisation climbed to 97.5%, a recent high, and stocks at the Cushing delivery hub fell by 534,000 barrels to 25.6 million. US crude production rose to a record 14.065 million barrels a day, and the Strategic Petroleum Reserve fell by a further 2.9 million barrels to 273.5 million. The EIA report was brought forward to Wednesday morning because of the Thanksgiving holiday; the American Petroleum Institute's report the day before had shown crude stocks up by just 126,000 barrels.

**5. OPEC+: Russia says the capacity review will be finished by year-end; Iraq still wants its capacity set at 5.2 million barrels a day as Sunday's meeting sets the 2027 framework.** Russian Deputy Prime Minister Alexander Novak said on Wednesday that OPEC+'s review of members' production capacities will be completed by the end of the year; the review will set the basis for each member's 2027 production quotas. Iraq is still pressing for a higher capacity estimate: OPEC's first-ever independent review assessed Iraq at 4.9 million barrels a day, and Iraq's oil minister has written to OPEC insisting the country can reach 5.2 million barrels a day. All 21 OPEC+ countries meet on Sunday, November 29, to decide the 2027 production framework, and production cuts of about 2 million barrels a day expire at the end of the year.

**6. China and India set for record Russian oil imports this year: together they now buy more than 80% of Russia's crude exports.** Reuters reports, citing tanker data, that seaborne Russian oil shipments to China and India have averaged 3.4 million barrels a day this year through mid-November, about 530,000 barrels a day more than a year earlier, and that the two countries together now buy more than 80% of Russia's crude exports. China imported 2.34 million barrels a day of Russian oil in January–October, up 17% and worth $59.6 billion, about a quarter of China's total crude imports; India averaged 1.99 million barrels a day of Russian crude through early November, up 250,000 barrels a day year on year, with Russian oil making up about 38% of India's crude imports in January–October. The increase reflects the supply gap left by the Iran war, temporary US waivers easing Russian oil sanctions, and Ukrainian drone strikes curbing Russian refining. Analysts say India's Russian purchases may fall back to pre-war levels next year if Middle East supplies return to normal.
