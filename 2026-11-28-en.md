**2. Hormuz: Iran says another tanker hit a mine on Friday; 17 tankers attacked in November, a Hong Kong-flagged tanker caught fire and one seafarer was seriously hurt.** Iran's Revolutionary Guards navy said on Friday that two tankers tried to pass north of Ghavam Island on an "unauthorised route" through the strait, one of them exploding after hitting a sea mine and the other being forced to turn back; the claim has not been independently verified, and no vessel names or casualties were given. A November 26 advisory from the Joint Maritime Information Center said 17 tankers have been struck in the Strait of Hormuz since the beginning of November, with the threat level held at Severe. On Thursday, the Hong Kong-flagged chemical and products tanker Kun Peng was struck off Oman, starting a fire that was later extinguished, and one seafarer was seriously injured; ADNOC Logistics & Services confirmed the death of a seafarer who had been missing after an earlier attack. Despite the rising risk, tanker traffic has increased rather than fallen: energy analytics firm Vortexa reports the seven-day average of crude, products and LNG vessels transiting the strait stayed above 26 through November, higher than in October.

**3. OPEC+: three sources say Sunday's meeting is likely to keep current output targets in place into the first quarter of 2027.** Reuters reports, citing three sources, that OPEC+ is set to keep first-quarter 2027 output targets unchanged at Sunday's meeting, as the Iran war continues to disrupt Middle East exports and disagreements persist over the capacity review; no decision has yet been reached and talks continue. Iraq is still pushing for a higher capacity assessment: the independent review put its capacity at 4.9 million barrels a day, while Baghdad insists it can reach 5.2 million. OPEC+ ministers will begin a series of four meetings on Sunday at 1300 GMT to decide 2027 output policy, and production cuts of about 2 million barrels a day expire at the end of the year. Rystad Energy expects a global supply surplus averaging around 5 million barrels a day next year, even with only a partial recovery in Gulf flows.

**4. Trump: victory over Iran could come "whenever I want it to be"; the US would hit Pickaxe Mountain if it acts again; Israel says it is ready to strike Iran alone at any moment.** President Trump told US troops in Thanksgiving calls on Thursday that victory over Iran was "at hand" and would come "whenever, sort of whenever I want it to be, to be honest," while saying he was still giving Tehran "every chance to make a deal and save a lot of lives." Speaking to reporters afterwards, he said Iran's military had been "decimated" and that if Washington took further action, "we would absolutely hit Pickaxe Mountain" — the tunnel complex Iran has been digging near its Natanz uranium enrichment plant — though the US has not seen any real activity there. An Israeli military spokesman said the same day that Israel is ready to strike Iran "at any moment," even without US forces.

**5. Iran: navy says it recovered two tankers seized by the US; 26 more tugboats added to its "non-compliance" list, bringing the total to 266.** Iranian Navy commander Shahram Irani claimed his forces boarded and recovered two Iranian tankers the US military had "stolen," without naming the ships or saying when; Washington has not commented. Iran's Persian Gulf Strait Authority added 26 tugboats to its non-compliance list on November 24 for providing services to vessels it says are operating in violation of Iranian restrictions, bringing the list to 266 vessels. The Joint Maritime Information Center clarified that the list is a unilateral Iranian designation rather than an internationally recognised sanctions list. Iran is pressing passing ships to file permits with the authority, follow Iranian-designated routes and buy insurance from state-backed providers.

**6. US: oil rigs rise to 470, a 1.5-year high; crude output sets a fifth straight weekly record.** Baker Hughes reported that the number of active US oil rigs rose by one to 470 in the week ended November 25, the highest since May 2025; gas rigs fell by one to 127, leaving the total rig count unchanged at 607, up 63 from a year earlier. EIA data show US crude production rose to 14.065 million barrels a day in the week ended November 20, a record for a fifth straight week. The rig report was released on Wednesday, ahead of the Thanksgiving holiday.
